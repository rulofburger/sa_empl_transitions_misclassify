\section{A Unified EM Framework for Misclassified Employment Dynamics with Tenure and Nonemployment Durations}

\subsection{States, sampling, and notation}
We observe individuals \(i=1,\dots,N\) at \(t\in\{1,2,3\}\) (quarters). The \emph{true} employment state is \(s_{it}^*\in\{0,1\}\), where 1 denotes employment. The \emph{observed} state is \(s_{it}\in\{0,1\}\), measured with misclassification. Let the latent 3–period history be
\[
H_i=(h_{i1},h_{i2},h_{i3})\in\{0,1\}^3,\qquad h_{it}\equiv s_{it}^*.
\]
We write \(h=(h_1,h_2,h_3)\) when individual indices are inessential, and denote the set of the eight histories by \(\mathcal{H}\).

We also observe \emph{tenure} (employment duration) \(g_{it}\ge 0\) and \emph{nonemployment duration} \(d_{it}\ge 0\). At any \(t\), only one of \((g_{it},d_{it})\) is observed, determined by \(s_{it}\): if \(s_{it}=1\) we observe \(g_{it}\), if \(s_{it}=0\) we observe \(d_{it}\). Survey weights are \(w_i>0\).

\subsection{True dynamics: discrete- and continuous-time views}
\paragraph{Discrete-time Markov chain.} The true chain \(\{s_{t}^*\}\) is first-order Markov with
\[
\alpha\equiv \Pr(s_{1}^*=1),\qquad
\theta_1\equiv \Pr(s_{t}^*=1\mid s_{t-1}^*=1),\qquad
\theta_0\equiv \Pr(s_{t}^*=1\mid s_{t-1}^*=0).
\]
Hence the prior mass on a history \(h\in\mathcal{H}\) is
\begin{equation}
p(H=h\mid\alpha,\theta_0,\theta_1)=
\alpha^{h_1}(1-\alpha)^{1-h_1}\prod_{t=2}^3
\Big[\theta_1^{\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=1,h_t=1\}}(1-\theta_1)^{\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=1,h_t=0\}}
\theta_0^{\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=0,h_t=1\}}(1-\theta_0)^{\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=0,h_t=0\}}\Big].
\label{eq:priorH}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Duration model and exponential spell lengths.}
We model \emph{true} spell lengths as exponential random variables:
\(
G\sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda_g),\quad D\sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda_d),
\)
where \(\lambda_g>0\) governs the rate at which employment spells end and \(\lambda_d>0\) governs the rate at which nonemployment spells end. The exponential is a natural starting point because it arises from a two-state continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC).

\paragraph{CTMC motivation.}
If the underlying process were a two-state CTMC with intensities \(\alpha_1\) (job loss, \(1\to 0\)) and \(\alpha_0\) (job entry, \(0\to 1\)), then with quarterly spacing \(\Delta=3\) months, the discrete-time transition probabilities and the continuous-time intensities would be related by
\begin{equation}
\theta_1 = 1 - e^{-\alpha_1\cdot 3},\qquad
\theta_0 = 1 - e^{-\alpha_0\cdot 3},
\label{eq:ctmc_link}
\end{equation}
and the memoryless property would imply \(\lambda_g=\alpha_1\), \(\lambda_d=\alpha_0\), giving the deterministic ``linked'' relationship
\begin{equation}
\lambda_g = -\tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\theta_1),\qquad
\lambda_d = -\tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\theta_0).
\label{eq:lambdas_link}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Free specification (primary).}
In practice, the CTMC memoryless property rarely holds for employment data. Real employment spells exhibit positive duration dependence: longer spells tend to be more stable, producing tenure distributions with heavy right tails that are inconsistent with the exponential implied by any single transition probability. Imposing \eqref{eq:lambdas_link} forces a single parameter \(\theta_1\) to simultaneously match the quarterly transition counts (which want \(\theta_1\) high, e.g.\ 0.97) and the tenure distribution (which requires \(\lambda_g\) to be small, pulling \(\theta_1\) down). This creates an irreconcilable tension in estimation.

We therefore adopt the \textbf{free specification}: the exponential rates \(\lambda_g, \lambda_d\) are estimated as independent parameters, separate from the transition probabilities \(\theta_0, \theta_1\). The transition probabilities govern the \emph{Markov prior} over latent histories \eqref{eq:priorH}, while the exponential rates govern the \emph{duration emission} densities. Each is identified from its own part of the data (Section~\ref{sec:identification}).

The CTMC link \eqref{eq:lambdas_link} is retained as a \emph{testable restriction}: after fitting the free model, one can test \(H_0: \lambda_g = -\tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\theta_1)\) via a likelihood ratio test (Section~\ref{sec:ctmc_test}).

\subsection{Deterministic clocks for tenure and nonemployment}
Given a history \(h\), the \emph{true} tenure and nonemployment clocks evolve deterministically at quarterly grid points:
\begin{equation}
g_t^*(h)=h_t\big(g_{t-1}^*(h)+\tfrac{1}{4}\big),\quad g_1^*\sim Exp(\lambda_g) ;\qquad
d_t^*(h)=(1-h_t)\big(d_{t-1}^*(h)+\tfrac{1}{4}\big),\quad d_1^*\sim Exp(\lambda_d).
\label{eq:deterministic_clocks}
\end{equation}
Thus a \emph{continuation} \(h_{t-1}=h_t=1\) increments tenure by \(0.25\), and a \emph{start} \(h_{t-1}=0,h_t=1\) resets tenure to \(0.25\). Analogously for nonemployment.

\subsection{Observation model: misclassification and duration measurement}
\paragraph{State misclassification.}
Observed states are misclassified symmetrically with probability \(\pi\):
\begin{equation}
q_{ab}(\pi)\equiv \Pr(s_{t}=a\mid s_{t}^*=b)=
\pi\mathbf{1}\{a\neq b\}+(1-\pi)\mathbf{1}\{a=b\},\qquad a,b\in\{0,1\}.
\label{eq:q_misclass}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Duration measurement: different variances by regime.}
When \(s_{t}=s_{t}^*\) (correct classification), we observe the \emph{appropriate} clock with additive Gaussian noise; at spell \emph{starts} the marginal law is exGaussian (EMG), and at \emph{continuations} we use Gaussian \emph{increments}. We allow different measurement variances in employment and nonemployment:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^g_{t}\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_g^2),\qquad
\varepsilon^d_{t}\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_d^2).
\label{eq:regime_sigmas}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Matched employment (\(s_{t}=h_t=1\)).}

\item Start (\(h_{t-1}=0,h_t=1\)): \(g_{t}\) assume the full quarter, with normal noise component,
\[
g_{t} -0.25 = \varepsilon^g_{t},
\]
with pdf \(N(0.25;\sigma_g^2)\).



\item Continuation (\(h_{t-1}=h_t=1\)): the increment
\begin{equation}
\Delta^g_{t} \equiv (g_{t}-g_{t-1}) - \tfrac{1}{4} \ \sim\ \mathcal{N}\!\big(0,\,2\sigma_g^2\big),
\label{eq:emp_increment}
\end{equation}
independently over \(t\) conditional on the history.

\item Employed from the start, (\(h_{1}=1\)): \(g_{t}\) follows the EMG density,
\[
g_{t} = X_{t} + \varepsilon^g_{t},\qquad X_{t}\sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda_g )
\]
\end{itemize}




\textbf{Matched nonemployment (\(s_{t}=h_t=0\)).}

Starts and continuations use

\begin{equation}
\Delta^d_{t} \equiv (d_{t}-d_{t-1}) - \tfrac{1}{4} \ \sim\ \mathcal{N}\!\big(0,\,2\sigma_d^2\big).
\label{eq:nonemp_increment}
\end{equation}

While initially nonemployed are EMG with \((\lambda_d,\sigma_d^2)\)

\textbf{Misclassified durations (\(s_{t}\neq h_t\)).}

If \(h_t=1,s_{t}=0\) (true employment, observed nonemployment), the \emph{inappropriate} duration \(D\sim\mathrm{EMG}(\lambda_d, \sigma_d^2)\). If \(h_t=0,s_{t}=1\), then \(G\sim\mathrm{EMG}(\lambda_g, \sigma_g^2)\).

\subsection{Observed-data likelihood}
Let \(y_i=(s_{i1},s_{i2},s_{i3},g_{i1},g_{i2},g_{i3},d_{i1},d_{i2},d_{i3})\). Conditional on \(h\),
\[
p(y_i\mid h;\Theta)=\underbrace{\prod_{t=1}^3 q_{s_{it},h_t}(\pi)}_{\text{state misclassification}}\times
\underbrace{\prod_{t=1}^3 \ell_t(y_{it}\mid s_{it},h_{t-1},h_t;\lambda_g,\lambda_d,\sigma_g^2,\sigma_d^2)}_{\text{duration emission}},
\]
where \(\ell_t\) selects the EMG/Normal-increment/Exponential factor appropriate to \((s_{it},h_{t-1},h_t)\) using \eqref{eq:emp_increment}–\eqref{eq:nonemp_increment}. The prior over histories is \eqref{eq:priorH}. With survey weights \(\{w_i\}\), the observed-data log-likelihood is
\begin{equation}
\ell(\Theta)=\sum_{i=1}^N w_i\log\bigg[\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}} p(H_i=h\mid\alpha,\theta_0,\theta_1)\,
\prod_{t=1}^3 q_{s_{it},h_t}(\pi)\,
\prod_{t=1}^3 \ell_t(\cdot)\bigg],
\label{eq:obs_ll}
\end{equation}
with parameters
\[
\Theta=(\alpha,\theta_0,\theta_1,\pi,\sigma_g^2,\lambda_g,\lambda_d).
\]
In the free specification \(\lambda_g\) and \(\lambda_d\) are estimated independently. Under the linked (CTMC) restriction, \(\lambda_g\) and \(\lambda_d\) are deterministic functions of \((\theta_0,\theta_1)\) via \eqref{eq:lambdas_link}, reducing the free parameter count by two.

\textbf{Remark on nonemployment measurement variance.} In our application, nonemployment duration is observed as a categorical variable (7~bins). The only source of measurement error on the timegap side is state misclassification --- being placed in the wrong bin entirely. There is no continuous measurement noise \(\sigma_d^2\) to estimate. On the employment side, tenure is observed as a continuous variable, so \(\sigma_g^2\) captures reporting noise in tenure levels.

Note, this is the standard mixture likelihood for the mixture log-likelihood. 

We assume the following conditional independence:

\begin{itemize}
    \item State misclassifications are independent across periods $t$ \\
    \item Duration emissions are independent across periods $t$ \\
    \item State misclassifications and duration emissions (i.e. observed data generated/emitted by latent state) are independent
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Complete-data formulation}
The observed data likelihood is difficult to optimize due to the sum over latent histories. We follow the the EM-algorithm approach. The complete-data likelihood formulation assumes we know which history, $h$, generated each observation.

Therefore, introduce indicators \(z_{ih}=\mathbf{1}\{H_i=h\}\) with \(\sum_h z_{ih}=1\). Then we have 
\[L_c(\Theta) = \prod_{i=1}^N \prod_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \left[ p(H_i=h\mid\alpha,\theta_0,\theta_1) \prod_{t=1}^3 q_{s_{it},h_t}(\pi) \prod_{t=1}^3 \ell_t(\cdot) \right]^{w_i z_{ih}}\]

Taking the log gives the complete-data log-likelihood as:
\begin{align}
\log L_c(\Theta)
&=\sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}} w_i z_{ih}\bigg\{\log p(H_i=h\mid\alpha,\theta_0,\theta_1)
+\sum_{t=1}^3\log q_{s_{it},h_t}(\pi)+\sum_{t=1}^3\log \ell_t(\cdot)\bigg\}.
\label{eq:complete_ll}
\end{align}

The three terms in \eqref{eq:complete_ll} correspond to (i) the Markov prior over histories, (ii) state misclassification, and (iii) duration emissions. Crucially, under the \textbf{free specification}, these three blocks can be maximised \emph{independently}: the Markov prior depends on \((\alpha, \theta_0, \theta_1)\), misclassification depends only on \(\pi\), and the emission block depends on \((\sigma_g^2, \lambda_g, \lambda_d)\). This clean separation is the key advantage of the free specification --- each parameter is updated from its own part of the data, avoiding the conflicting signals that arise under the linked specification (where \(\theta\) appears in both the prior and emission blocks through the CTMC link).

\paragraph{E-step.}

Starting from \(\Theta^{(0)}\), iterate E- and M-steps until convergence of \(\ell(\Theta)\).

Compute responsibilities (posterior probabilities of histories):
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{ih}\equiv \mathbb{E}[z_{ih}\mid y_i;\Theta^{\text{old}}]
=\frac{p(H_i=h\mid\alpha^{\text{old}},\theta_0^{\text{old}},\theta_1^{\text{old}})\prod_{t=1}^3 q_{s_{it},h_t}(\pi^{\text{old}})\prod_{t=1}^3\ell_t(y_{it}\mid s_{it},h_{t-1},h_t;\Theta^{\text{old}})}
{\sum_{h'\in\mathcal{H}} p(H_i=h'\mid\alpha^{\text{old}},\theta_0^{\text{old}},\theta_1^{\text{old}})\prod_{t=1}^3 q_{s_{it},h'_t}(\pi)\prod_{t=1}^3\ell_t(y_{it}\mid s_{it},h'_{t-1},h'_t;\Theta^{\text{old}})},
\label{eq:resp}
\end{equation}
where \(\ell_t(y_{it}\mid s_{it},h_{t-1},h_t;\Theta)\) is the duration emission density that depends on the observation type:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Wave~1 matched employment} (\(t=1\), \(h_1=1\), \(s_{1}=1\)):
\[
\ell_1(\cdot) = f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(g_{1};\lambda_g,\sigma_g^2),
\]


\item \textbf{Wave~1 matched nonemployment} (\(t=1\), \(h_1=0\), \(s_{1}=0\)):
\[
\ell_1(\cdot) = f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(d_{1};\lambda_d,\sigma_d^2).
\]
\item \textbf{Employment continuation, previous observed} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=1\), \(h_t=1\), \(s_{it}=1\), \(s_{i,t-1}=1\)):
\[
\ell_t(\cdot) = \phi\!\left(\frac{\Delta^g_{t}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma_g}\right)\Big/\!\big(\sqrt{2}\sigma_g\big),
\quad\text{where}\quad \Delta^g_{t} = (g_{t}-g_{t-1}) - 0.25.
\]

\item \textbf{Employment continuation, previous misclassified} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=1\), \(h_t=1\), \(s_{t}=1\), \(s_{t-1}=0\)):
\[
\ell_t(\cdot) = f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(g_{t};\lambda_g,\sigma_g^2).
\]

\item \textbf{Nonemployment continuation, previous observed} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=0\), \(h_t=0\), \(s_{t}=0\), \(s_{t-1}=0\)):
\[
\ell_t(\cdot) = \phi\!\left(\frac{\Delta^d_{t}}{\sqrt{2}\sigma_d}\right)\Big/\!\big(\sqrt{2}\sigma_d\big),
\quad\text{where}\quad \Delta^d_{t} = (d_{t}-d_{t-1}) - 0.25.
\]

\item \textbf{Nonemployment continuation, previous misclassified} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=0\), \(h_t=0\), \(s_{t}=0\), \(s_{t-1}=1\)):
\[
\ell_t(\cdot) = f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(d_{t};\lambda_d,\sigma_d^2).
\]


\item \textbf{Within-panel employment start} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=0\), \(h_t=1\), \(s_{it}=1\)):
\[
\ell_t(\cdot) = \phi\!\left(\frac{g_{t}-0.25}{\sigma_g}\right)\Big/\sigma_g.
\]

\item \textbf{Within-panel nonemployment start} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=1\), \(h_t=0\), \(s_{t}=0\)):
\[
\ell_t(\cdot) = \phi\!\left(\frac{d_{t}-0.25}{\sigma_d}\right)\Big/\sigma_d.
\]

\item \textbf{Misclassified as employed} (\(h_t=0\), \(s_{t}=1\)):
\[
\ell_t(\cdot) = f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(g_{t};\lambda_g,\sigma_g^2).
\]

\item \textbf{Misclassified as nonemployed} (\(h_t=1\), \(s_{t}=0\)):
\[
\ell_t(\cdot) = f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(d_{t};\lambda_d,\sigma_d^2).
\]
\end{itemize}

Under the \textbf{free specification}, \(\lambda_g\) and \(\lambda_d\) are independent parameters updated in the M-step from the duration emission data. Under the \textbf{linked specification} (testable restriction), they are computed from the transition probabilities via \eqref{eq:lambdas_link} at each iteration.


\medskip

Accumulate weighted sufficient statistics using \(w_i\gamma_{ih}\) as weights:

\textbf{Markov transition statistics:}
\begin{align}
C_1 &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\,h_1,\qquad C_0=\sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}(1-h_1),\nonumber\\
D_1 &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3 \mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=1\},\quad
D_0 = \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3 \mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=0\},\nonumber\\
T_{11} &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3 \mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=1,h_t=1\},\quad
T_{01} = \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3 \mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=0,h_t=1\},\nonumber\\
M &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=1}^3 \mathbf{1}\{s_{it}\neq h_t\}. \label{eq:suff_trans}
\end{align}

\textbf{Duration variance statistics:}

Two sources of information identify the measurement variances \(\sigma_g^2\) and \(\sigma_d^2\): continuation increments (where both current and lagged durations are observed) and within-panel starts (where the level is observed with known mean).

\emph{Employment continuations} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=h_t=1\), \(s_{it}=1\), \(s_{i,t-1}=1\)):
\begin{align}
S_g &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=h_t=1,\,s_{it}=1,\,s_{i,t-1}=1\} \big(\Delta^g_{it}\big)^2,\nonumber\\
N_g &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=h_t=1,\,s_{it}=1,\,s_{i,t-1}=1\},
\label{eq:suff_gauss_g}
\end{align}
where \(\Delta^g_{it} = (g_{it}-g_{i,t-1}) - 0.25\) from \eqref{eq:emp_increment}. The condition \(s_{i,t-1}=1\) is required so that the lagged tenure \(g_{i,t-1}\) is observed. When the previous wave is misclassified (\(s_{i,t-1}=0\)), the increment is undefined and that observation is excluded. The efficiency loss is proportional to \(\pi\) and small. The estimator remains unbiased because \(\sigma_g^2\) is independent of classification at \(t-1\).

\emph{Nonemployment continuations} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=h_t=0\), \(s_{it}=0\), \(s_{i,t-1}=0\)):
\begin{align}
S_d &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=h_t=0,\,s_{it}=0,\,s_{i,t-1}=0\} \big(\Delta^d_{it}\big)^2,\nonumber\\
N_d &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=h_t=0,\,s_{it}=0,\,s_{i,t-1}=0\},
\label{eq:suff_gauss_d}
\end{align}
where \(\Delta^d_{it} = (d_{it}-d_{i,t-1}) - 0.25\) from \eqref{eq:nonemp_increment}. The same logic applies: \(s_{i,t-1}=0\) ensures the lagged nonemployment duration is observed.

\emph{Within-panel employment starts} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=0\), \(h_t=1\), \(s_{it}=1\)):
\begin{align}
S_g^{\mathrm{start}} &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=0,\,h_t=1,\,s_{it}=1\}\big(g_{it}-0.25\big)^2,\nonumber\\
N_g^{\mathrm{start}} &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=0,\,h_t=1,\,s_{it}=1\}.
\label{eq:suff_gauss_g_start}
\end{align}

\emph{Within-panel nonemployment starts} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=1\), \(h_t=0\), \(s_{it}=0\)):
\begin{align}
S_d^{\mathrm{start}} &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=1,\,h_t=0,\,s_{it}=0\}\big(d_{it}-0.25\big)^2,\nonumber\\
N_d^{\mathrm{start}} &= \sum_{i,h} w_i\gamma_{ih}\sum_{t=2}^3\mathbf{1}\{h_{t-1}=1,\,h_t=0,\,s_{it}=0\}.
\label{eq:suff_gauss_d_start}
\end{align}

\textbf{Remark on within-panel starts.} Within-panel starts follow \(N(0.25, \sigma^2)\) and yield closed-form sufficient statistics \((S_g^{\mathrm{start}}, N_g^{\mathrm{start}}, S_d^{\mathrm{start}}, N_d^{\mathrm{start}})\), which are pooled with the continuation statistics in the M-step for \(\sigma_g^2\) and \(\sigma_d^2\).

\textbf{EMG sufficient statistics for the \(\lambda\) update.}

Under the free specification, the EMG emission terms depend on \(\lambda_g\) (and symmetrically \(\lambda_d\)) but \emph{not} on \(\theta_1\) or \(\theta_0\). The E-step accumulates the weighted EMG observations needed by the M-step.

The EMG log-density is
\begin{equation}
\log f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(x; \lambda, \sigma^2) = \log \lambda + \tfrac{\lambda^2 \sigma^2}{2} - \lambda x + \log \Phi\!\left(\frac{x - \lambda\sigma^2}{\sigma}\right) + \mathrm{const},
\label{eq:log_emg}
\end{equation}
where \(\Phi(\cdot)\) is the standard Normal CDF. Its derivative with respect to \(\lambda\) is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \log f_{\mathrm{EMG}}}{\partial \lambda} = \frac{1}{\lambda} + \lambda\sigma^2 - x - \sigma\,\frac{\phi(u)}{\Phi(u)},
\label{eq:demg_dlambda}
\end{equation}
where \(u = (x - \lambda\sigma^2)/\sigma\) and \(\phi(u)/\Phi(u)\) is the inverse Mills ratio.

Three observation types contribute EMG terms involving \(\lambda_g\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Wave~1 matched employment} (\(t=1\), \(h_1=1\), \(s_{i1}=1\)): contributes \(\log f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(g_{i1}; \lambda_g, \sigma_g^2)\).
\item \textbf{Employment continuation, previous misclassified} (\(t\ge 2\), \(h_{t-1}=1\), \(h_t=1\), \(s_{it}=1\), \(s_{i,t-1}=0\)): contributes \(\log f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(g_{it}; \lambda_g, \sigma_g^2)\).
\item \textbf{Misclassified as employed} (\(h_t=0\), \(s_{it}=1\)): contributes \(\log f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(g_{it}; \lambda_g, \sigma_g^2)\).
\end{enumerate}
Let \(\mathcal{E}_g\) denote the set of all \((i,t,h)\) triples belonging to these three types. The E-step collects the pairs \((x_{it}, w_i\gamma_{ih})\) for all elements of \(\mathcal{E}_g\) and passes them to the M-step for the \(\lambda_g\) update. The corresponding three types for \(\lambda_d\) are: wave~1 matched nonemployment; nonemployment continuation with previous misclassified; and misclassified as nonemployed.


\paragraph{M-step (free specification).}
The E-step computes the responsibilities \(\gamma_{ih}\) via \eqref{eq:resp}. The M-step maximises the expected complete-data log-likelihood \(Q(\Theta\mid\Theta^{\text{old}})\), which amounts to replacing \(z_{ih}\) by \(\gamma_{ih}\) in \eqref{eq:complete_ll}.

The key advantage of the free specification is that \(Q\) decomposes into blocks that can be maximised \emph{independently}. We derive each update in turn.

\textbf{Initial-state probability:}
\begin{equation}
\hat{\alpha}=\frac{C_1}{C_0+C_1}.
\label{eq:mstep_alpha}
\end{equation}
In words: the fraction of (weighted, responsibility-adjusted) individuals who are truly employed at wave~1. If stationarity is imposed, set \(\alpha \leftarrow \theta_0/(\theta_0+1-\theta_1)\) instead.

\textbf{Transition probabilities (closed form):}

Under the free specification, \(\theta_1\) and \(\theta_0\) appear \emph{only} in the Markov prior block of \(Q\). They do not appear in the emission block (because \(\lambda_g\) and \(\lambda_d\) are separate parameters). The M-step is therefore the standard closed-form MLE for a Markov chain, weighted by responsibilities:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\theta}_1 = \frac{T_{11}}{D_1}, \qquad \hat{\theta}_0 = \frac{T_{01}}{D_0}.
\label{eq:mstep_theta_free}
\end{equation}
In words: \(\hat{\theta}_1\) is the fraction of ``from-employed'' periods that result in continued employment, and \(\hat{\theta}_0\) is the fraction of ``from-nonemployed'' periods that result in employment. These are exactly the formulas that the simple binary-sequence MLE uses --- the only difference is that the counts are soft (weighted by responsibilities \(\gamma_{ih}\)) rather than hard.

\textbf{Misclassification:}
\begin{equation}
\hat{\pi}=\frac{M}{3\sum_i w_i}.
\label{eq:mstep_pi}
\end{equation}
In words: the average (weighted) fraction of wave-observations where the observed state disagrees with the latent history.

\textbf{Measurement variance for tenure (pooling continuations and starts):}

This is unchanged from the general derivation. Continuations contribute \(\Delta^g_{it}\sim N(0,2\sigma_g^2)\) and starts contribute \((g_{it}-0.25)\sim N(0,\sigma_g^2)\). The pooled MLE is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\sigma}_g^2=\frac{S_g + 2\,S_g^{\mathrm{start}}}{2(N_g + N_g^{\mathrm{start}})}.
\label{eq:mstep_sigma_g}
\end{equation}
The factor of 2 on \(S^{\mathrm{start}}\) rescales start contributions (variance \(\sigma^2\)) to the continuation scale (variance \(2\sigma^2\)). Enforce a small floor if needed.

\textbf{Exponential rate for tenure \(\lambda_g\) (Brent's method):}

Under the free specification, \(\lambda_g\) appears only in the EMG emission terms. The M-step maximises:
\begin{equation}
Q_{\mathrm{EMG}}(\lambda_g) = \sum_{(i,t,h)\in\mathcal{E}_g} w_i\gamma_{ih}\,\log f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(x_{it};\lambda_g,\hat{\sigma}_g^2),
\label{eq:Q_lambda_g_free}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{E}_g\) collects all EMG-type observations for the employment side (wave-1 matched, continuation with previous misclassified, and misclassified-as-employed). The first-order condition is
\begin{equation}
\sum_{(i,t,h)\in\mathcal{E}_g} w_i\gamma_{ih}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda_g} + \lambda_g\hat{\sigma}_g^2 - x_{it} - \hat{\sigma}_g\,\frac{\phi(u_{it})}{\Phi(u_{it})}\right] = 0,
\label{eq:foc_lambda_g}
\end{equation}
where \(u_{it} = (x_{it} - \lambda_g\hat{\sigma}_g^2)/\hat{\sigma}_g\). This is a one-dimensional root-finding problem in \(\lambda_g\), solved by Brent's method on the interval \((\varepsilon, \lambda_{\max})\). Convergence typically requires 5--15 iterations.

Note: because \(\hat{\sigma}_g^2\) is already computed (from the step above) and is \emph{independent} of \(\lambda_g\), the EMG log-likelihood \eqref{eq:Q_lambda_g_free} is concave in \(\lambda_g\) given \(\hat{\sigma}_g^2\), guaranteeing a unique root.

\textbf{Exponential rate for nonemployment \(\lambda_d\) (interval-censored MLE):}

Nonemployment duration is observed as a discrete category \(k\in\{1,\dots,7\}\) corresponding to intervals \([a_k, b_k)\). Under the exponential model, the probability of observing category \(k\) is
\[
P(D\in[a_k,b_k)\mid\lambda_d) = e^{-\lambda_d a_k} - e^{-\lambda_d b_k}.
\]
The M-step maximises the weighted interval-censored log-likelihood:
\begin{equation}
Q_{\mathrm{cat}}(\lambda_d) = \sum_{(i,t,h)\in\mathcal{E}_d} w_i\gamma_{ih}\,\log\!\big(e^{-\lambda_d a_{k_{it}}} - e^{-\lambda_d b_{k_{it}}}\big),
\label{eq:Q_lambda_d_free}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{E}_d\) collects all interval-censored observations for the nonemployment side and \(k_{it}\) is the observed category at wave \(t\). The first-order condition
\begin{equation}
\sum_{(i,t,h)\in\mathcal{E}_d} w_i\gamma_{ih}\,\frac{-a_{k_{it}} e^{-\lambda_d a_{k_{it}}} + b_{k_{it}} e^{-\lambda_d b_{k_{it}}}}{e^{-\lambda_d a_{k_{it}}} - e^{-\lambda_d b_{k_{it}}}} = 0
\label{eq:foc_lambda_d}
\end{equation}
is solved by Brent's method on \((\varepsilon, \lambda_{\max})\). This is concave in \(\lambda_d\) (standard exponential family result), so the root is unique.


\paragraph{M-step under the linked specification (testable restriction).}
\label{sec:ctmc_test}

Under the linked (CTMC) specification, one imposes the restrictions \(\lambda_g = -\tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\theta_1)\) and \(\lambda_d = -\tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\theta_0)\). This means \(\theta_1\) enters \emph{both} the Markov prior block and the EMG emission block. The M-step can no longer update \(\theta\) in closed form; instead, one solves a joint first-order condition by Brent's method:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\frac{T_{11}}{\theta_1} - \frac{D_1 - T_{11}}{1-\theta_1}}_{\text{from } Q_{\mathrm{prior}}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{3(1-\theta_1)} \cdot \sum_{(i,t,h)\in\mathcal{E}_g} w_i\gamma_{ih}\,\frac{\partial \log f_{\mathrm{EMG}}}{\partial \lambda_g}}_{\text{from } Q_{\mathrm{emission}}} \;=\; 0.
\label{eq:foc_theta1_linked}
\end{equation}
An analogous equation holds for \(\theta_0\). Once \(\hat{\theta}_1, \hat{\theta}_0\) are obtained, the rates follow deterministically: \(\hat{\lambda}_g = -\tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\hat{\theta}_1)\), \(\hat{\lambda}_d = -\tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\hat{\theta}_0)\).

The linked specification reduces the parameter count by 2 (from 7 to 5 free parameters), but forces the transition probabilities and duration rates to satisfy the CTMC restriction. If this restriction does not hold in the data --- for example, because real employment spells exhibit positive duration dependence --- the joint FOC produces a ``compromise'' estimate that satisfies neither the transition data nor the duration data.

\textbf{Testing the CTMC restriction.} After fitting both the free and linked models, one can test
\[
H_0: \lambda_g = -\tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\theta_1) \;\text{and}\; \lambda_d = -\tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\theta_0)
\]
via a likelihood ratio test with 2 degrees of freedom: \(\mathrm{LR} = 2[\ell_{\text{free}} - \ell_{\text{linked}}] \sim \chi^2_2\) under \(H_0\). Rejection indicates that the CTMC memoryless property is violated and the free specification is preferred.


\paragraph{Iteration and convergence.}
Iterate \((\text{E-step},\text{M-step})\) until \(\ell(\Theta)\) in \eqref{eq:obs_ll} increases by less than a tolerance. Under the free specification, the E-step uses \eqref{eq:resp}; the M-step applies \eqref{eq:mstep_alpha}, \eqref{eq:mstep_theta_free}, \eqref{eq:mstep_pi}, \eqref{eq:mstep_sigma_g}, \eqref{eq:foc_lambda_g}, and \eqref{eq:foc_lambda_d}. Monotone ascent is guaranteed because each M-step block independently maximises its portion of the Q function: \(\alpha\), \(\theta_1\), \(\theta_0\), and \(\pi\) in closed form; \(\sigma_g^2\) in closed form; \(\lambda_g\) and \(\lambda_d\) via Brent's method on concave one-dimensional sub-problems.

\subsection{Identification under the free specification}
\label{sec:identification}

A natural concern when decoupling \(\lambda\) from \(\theta\) is whether the model remains identified. We show below that each parameter group is identified from a \emph{distinct} piece of the data, so there is no confounding or ridge between them. The argument proceeds in three steps.

\paragraph{Step 1: \(\sigma_g^2\) is identified from continuation increments.}
When an individual is truly employed at two consecutive waves (\(h_{t-1}=h_t=1\)) and correctly classified at both (\(s_{t-1}=s_t=1\)), the tenure increment is
\[
\Delta^g_t = (g_t - g_{t-1}) - 0.25 \sim N(0, 2\sigma_g^2).
\]
This random variable involves \emph{only} \(\sigma_g^2\) --- it does not depend on \(\lambda_g\), \(\theta_1\), \(\pi\), or any other parameter. The MLE is the familiar variance estimator \(\hat{\sigma}_g^2 = S_g / (2N_g)\) (plus the pooled within-panel start contribution). With \(N\approx 260{,}000\) observations and high persistence (\(\theta_1\approx 0.97\)), the vast majority of employed individuals contribute a continuation increment, providing abundant data for this estimate.

\paragraph{Step 2: Given \(\sigma_g^2\), \(\lambda_g\) is identified from EMG observations.}
The three EMG-type observations (wave-1 matched, continuation with previous misclassified, misclassified-as-employed) all follow the density \(f_{\mathrm{EMG}}(x; \lambda_g, \sigma_g^2)\). With \(\sigma_g^2\) pinned from Step~1, the EMG density has a single free parameter \(\lambda_g\). The log-likelihood is concave in \(\lambda_g\) given \(\sigma_g^2\) (because the EMG is a one-parameter exponential family in \(\lambda\) when \(\sigma^2\) is fixed), so the MLE for \(\lambda_g\) is unique.

Intuitively: if an individual reports 5~years of tenure at wave~1, the model asks ``what exponential rate \(\lambda_g\) makes this spell length most likely, given the known measurement noise \(\sigma_g^2\)?'' Long average tenure points to small \(\lambda_g\); short tenure points to large \(\lambda_g\). This is a standard exponential-rate estimation problem.

\paragraph{Step 3: \(\theta_1\) is identified from the Markov prior counts.}
The transition probabilities \(\theta_1, \theta_0\) appear only in the Markov prior \eqref{eq:priorH} and enter the Q function through the weighted transition counts \(T_{11}, D_1, T_{01}, D_0\). Under the free specification, the duration emissions contribute \emph{nothing} to the \(\theta\) update. The MLE is the standard closed-form estimator \(\hat{\theta}_1 = T_{11}/D_1\), \(\hat{\theta}_0 = T_{01}/D_0\), weighted by responsibilities.

\paragraph{Summary.} The three parameter groups \((\sigma_g^2)\), \((\lambda_g)\), and \((\theta_1)\) are identified from disjoint pieces of the likelihood: continuation increments, EMG level observations, and Markov transition counts, respectively. There is no ridge or confounding between them. The same argument applies to the nonemployment side (\(\lambda_d\) from interval-censored categories, \(\theta_0\) from Markov counts), except that there is no \(\sigma_d^2\) to estimate (the timegap is categorical with no continuous measurement noise).

\paragraph{Practical diagnostics.}
While the theoretical identification argument is clean, one may wish to verify identification empirically on a given dataset. Three diagnostics are available:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Multiple starts}: re-fit from \(K\) perturbed initial values. If all starts converge to the same log-likelihood and parameter values (within tolerance), identification is confirmed. If different starts yield similar log-likelihoods but different \((\lambda, \sigma^2)\) pairs, there is a ridge --- a sign of weak identification.
\item \textbf{Profile likelihood}: fix \(\lambda_g\) at a grid of values, re-optimise all other parameters, and plot the profile log-likelihood. A sharp peak indicates strong identification; a flat region indicates weakness.
\item \textbf{Numerical Hessian}: compute the observed information matrix at the MLE. If the eigenvalues corresponding to \(\lambda_g\) and \(\sigma_g^2\) are well-separated from zero, the parameters are locally identified. A near-zero eigenvalue signals a flat direction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Additional practical considerations}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep4pt
\item \emph{What identifies \(\pi\)?} Duration data provides strong identification of misclassification. If a person reports employment at all three waves but with tenure increments inconsistent with \(N(0,2\sigma_g^2)\), the model attributes this to misclassification rather than measurement noise. The EMG emission densities for misclassified observations have a different shape from the Normal densities used for correctly classified observations, allowing the two to be distinguished.
\item \emph{Overidentification.} With 3 waves there are \(2^3=8\) observed state patterns and continuous duration data at each wave. The free specification has 7 parameters \((\alpha,\theta_0,\theta_1,\pi,\sigma_g^2,\lambda_g,\lambda_d)\). The model is overidentified, which allows for specification testing (including the CTMC restriction test).
\item \emph{Asymmetric measurement model.} Employment tenure is observed as a continuous variable, so \(\sigma_g^2\) captures reporting noise. Nonemployment duration (timegap) is observed as a categorical variable (7~bins), so the only measurement error on the timegap side is state misclassification --- being placed in the wrong bin entirely. There is no continuous \(\sigma_d^2\) to estimate.
\item \emph{Increment likelihoods.} Using \eqref{eq:emp_increment}--\eqref{eq:nonemp_increment} differences out the unknown true duration level, leaving only measurement noise. This yields a clean \(N(0,2\sigma^2)\) density that identifies \(\sigma^2\) without dependence on \(\lambda\), and avoids using the same measurement error twice in consecutive level likelihoods.
\item \emph{Regularization.} Small floors on \(\sigma_g^2\) guard against pathological solutions typical in mixture-like settings. Transition caps (e.g., \(\theta_1\le 0.995\)) and rate bounds (e.g., \(\lambda_g \in [\varepsilon, 50]\)) can be imposed if needed.
\item \emph{Weights.} All sufficient statistics are weight-adjusted via \(w_i\gamma_{ih}\); likewise, the observed-data log-likelihood \eqref{eq:obs_ll} uses weights.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Never-worked population}

Individuals who have never held a job report no nonemployment duration because the survey question refers to time since last employment. We include these individuals by treating them as continuously nonemployed since the working-age threshold of 16~years. Their nonemployment duration at wave~\(t\) is \((a_{it} - 16)\times 12\) months, where \(a_{it}\) is age in years. This is mapped to a timegap category using the standard binning:
\begin{itemize}
\item Age 18: duration = 24 months, category 5 (\([12,36)\) months).
\item Age 21: duration = 60 months, category 7 (\([60,\infty)\) months).
\item Age 25+: duration \(\ge\) 108 months, category 7.
\end{itemize}
This assumption is conservative: the true nonemployment spell is at least as long as \((a_{it} - 16)\times 12\) months. The exponential model treats these as any other category-7 observation (contributing \(P(D\ge 5\text{ years}\mid\lambda_d) = e^{-\lambda_d\cdot 5}\) to the interval-censored likelihood), which helps pin down \(\lambda_d\) in the right tail. Excluding the never-worked population would bias the estimation sample toward shorter nonemployment spells.
