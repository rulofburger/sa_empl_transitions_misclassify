% --- Abstract ---
\begin{abstract}
Reliable estimates of employment transition rates are central to diagnosing the
nature of unemployment and designing effective labour market policy. Yet
classification error in survey responses to employment questions is pervasive
and, even when small, can inflate measured transition rates by an order of
magnitude. We develop a structural EM-algorithm estimator that uses three
consecutive waves of panel data to jointly identify true transition rates and
misclassification probabilities, requiring no reinterview or administrative
data. The estimator accommodates observed and unobserved worker heterogeneity,
second-order Markov dynamics, and tenure-based identification of classification
error. Applied to the South African Quarterly Labour Force Survey, we find a
misclassification probability of approximately 3\%, which causes conventional
methods to overstate quarterly employment entry and exit rates by a factor of
nearly three---from roughly 8\% to roughly 3\%. This finding is robust to
controlling for observable and unobservable worker heterogeneity, duration
dependence, and alternative matching algorithms. Survey respondents with
inconsistent age or education progressions exhibit substantially higher estimated
misclassification, providing direct mechanism evidence. Our results imply that
South African employment is considerably more persistent than previously
characterised, with important implications for the design of labour market
policy.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Misclassification error, employment transitions,
EM algorithm, panel data, South Africa

\noindent\textbf{JEL Codes:} J63, C23, C41
\end{abstract}
